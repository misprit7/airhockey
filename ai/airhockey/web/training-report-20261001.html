What changed

  • Expanded the simulated paddle-center workspace to give 30 mm rim clearance at the side and back rails. The forward boundary is 1200 mm in the physical grid frame.
  • Raised the simulated acceleration cap from 60 to 100 m/s²; retained the 12 m/s speed ceiling.
  • Added a mild position-based dwell cost near the rails. Edge visits are allowed; lingering accumulates a cost.
  • Refitted per-motor current and thermal dynamics from the post-replacement logs, retaining each drive’s measured RMS settings.
  • Practiced unpredictable fast releases and trained on successful neural corner-recovery attempts. Every runtime action still comes from one neural network.

Still one neural policy

The actor uses 16 frames of 42 physical features and its own three-way shot request: 675 inputs. Three 1,024-unit hidden layers produce six arrival-action outputs: position, velocity, arrival time, and acceleration effort.

The opposing player's requested shot is private. A separate value network gets training-only exercise context; that context does not enter the actor. The existing arrival decoder and motion guard enforce the simulated workspace and motion ceilings.

The actor started from possession-20260926-v3, with a training-only coordinate conversion for the larger action workspace. PPO continued on self-play, possession and defense exercises. The final corner-recovery pass imitated successful noisy rollouts from that learned actor while preserving its ordinary skills; no scripted recovery controller or scripted action labels were used.

Updated motor-load model

The model uses post-replacement characterization logs at nine holding locations and 1.5 mm pretension. It combines a spatial holding-current map with direction-dependent acceleration and speed terms, then integrates separate fast and slow load states for each motor.

All four motors have the same part family. Their logged drive settings still differ: fast RMS limits are approximately 4.1 / 5.8 / 4.1 / 5.8 A; slow limits are 4.0 / 4.1 / 4.0 / 4.1 A. The fit retains those measured settings. Holding behavior outside the surveyed area remains an explicit conservative estimate.

Limits of this result

  • Hot-start sustained load failures remain: 5/16 matches crossed the modeled threshold. The independent nominal cohort passed, but that does not establish universal safety.
  • The expanded physical travel area has not been probed in this session. No robot, camera or motors were activated.
  • The live runner still uses the previous workspace. This package is simulation-only and is rejected by the legacy physical adapter until workspace integration and physical qualification are completed.
  • Fast human shots can be impossible to reach after release; anticipatory positioning matters. These tests do not establish superhuman defense.
  • Difficult corner recovery remains inconsistent, and long rallies can still stall. Slow outgoing possession is reported separately so regressions are visible.
  • Rail contact projection fixes a simulation overlap artifact, but real paddle tilt, bounce, cable slack and corner holding currents still need measurements.
  • The 14–16 m/s subset did not improve over the previous policy (244 vs 245 saves in 342 trials). The aggregate defense gain comes from slower shots in the 10–16 m/s range.

Experiment selection

Checkpoint selection considered requested fast shots, moving-release saves, corner recovery, long-match thermal behavior and motion-cap audits. The latest training checkpoint was not automatically preferred. A fresh eight-match nominal cohort was evaluated after shortlisting; no further fitting used those fixtures.

  • The balanced PPO branch produced the strongest shooting results, but failed some long nominal and hot-load matches.
  • Longer coherent neural exploration and successful-trajectory imitation improved corner recovery without adding a tactical controller.
  • Stronger edge penalties, thermal urgency, forecast holding-load penalties and policy averaging were tested separately; their development and independent failures remain in the logs.
  • The final fast-release defense continuation put more weight on pre-release goal coverage and practiced 10–18 m/s shots. Its evaluated snapshots did not establish a better overall candidate.
